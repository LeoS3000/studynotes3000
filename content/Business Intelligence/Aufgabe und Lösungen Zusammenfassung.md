## **Introduction to Business Intelligence and Analytics (BI&A)**
* BI&A refers to the techniques, technologies, systems, practices, methodologies, and applications that analyze critical business data to help an enterprise better understand its business and market and make timely business decisions.
* BI&A has become an increasingly important concept for researchers and practitioners.
## **The Role of Absorptive Capacity in BI&A Value Creation**
* Absorptive capacity is the ability of a firm to recognize the value of new, external information, assimilate it, and apply it to commercial ends.
* Absorptive capacity plays a crucial role in transforming BI&A insights into valuable knowledge.
* Four capabilities of absorptive capacity:
	+ Acquisition: identifying and obtaining valuable data from external sources.
	+ Assimilation: processing internal and external data to capitalize on generated knowledge.
	+ Transformation: facilitating the internalization of newly acquired knowledge within an existing knowledge base.
	+ Exploitation: allowing transformed knowledge to be commercialized.
## **Technological, Human, and Relationship Assets**
* Technological assets: advanced analytics tools, data management systems, and IT infrastructure.
* Human assets: data scientists, business analysts, and personnel with domain-specific knowledge and IT expertise.
* Relationship assets: collaboration, communication, and strong decision-making culture.
## **Key Findings**
* Absorptive capacity is essential for transforming BI&A insights into valuable knowledge.
* Technological, human, and relationship assets support absorptive capacity capabilities.
* Companies need to develop a strong data culture and tolerance for mistakes to fully leverage BI&A potential.
* Aligning the organization's strategy with its data strategy is crucial for BI&A success.
## **Implications for Research and Practice**
* Future research should focus on the role of absorptive capacity in BI&A value creation.
* Practitioners should develop a comprehensive approach to BI&A, including technological, human, and relationship assets.
* Companies should prioritize developing a strong data culture, tolerance for mistakes, and a decision-making culture that blends analytics with intuition.

The article discusses the synergy between data integration (DI) and machine learning (ML). The authors, Xin Luna Dong and Theodoros Rekatsinas, argue that the increasing volume and variety of data have strengthened the connections between DI and ML. They highlight the importance of using data from various sources to train ML models and the need for accurate and clean data to achieve high-quality results.
The authors review three aspects of the synergy between DI and ML:
1. **DI solutions rely on ML-based approaches**: They discuss how recent advancements in ML have improved the results of DI tasks such as entity resolution, data fusion, and schema alignment.
2. **ML applications rely on DI**: They review how end-to-end ML applications rely on DI to identify accurate, clean, and relevant data for their analytics exercises.
3. **Open research challenges and opportunities**: They outline several open challenges and opportunities in the intersection of DI and ML, including multi-modal DI, fast and cheap training data for DI, human-in-the-loop DI, efficient model serving for DI, declarative interfaces for DI, and effective data augmentation for ML-pipelines.

# Stonebraker, M., & Ilyas, I. F. (2018). Data Integration: The Current Status and the Way Forward. IEEE Data Eng. Bull
**Introduction**
- Data integration is a challenging problem in large enterprises, where data is scattered across multiple silos.
- Traditional solutions, such as Master Data Management (MDM) systems, do not scale well.
- Machine learning (ML) is an obvious approach to scalability, but it requires careful consideration of practical issues.

**Scalable Data Integration**
- Traditional MDM systems do not scale well due to manual schema mapping and rule-based systems.
- Rule systems for classification and record linkage also do not scale.
- Machine learning can automate these steps, but it requires large amounts of labeled training data, which is scarce in enterprise settings.

**Challenges in Scalable Data Integration**
- Scarcity of training data: most ML models require large quantities of labeled data to learn the classification task.
- Lack of explainability: deep learning models are not explainable, which is a major impediment to adoption in enterprise settings.
- High provisioning and tuning cost: current data integration systems require highly trained personnel to author workflows and configure components.

**Future Directions**
- Data discovery: supporting data scientists in finding relevant data sets and navigating data lakes.
- Human involvement: making data integration systems easier to use and requiring less human interaction.
- Batch vs. exploratory workflows: supporting data scientists in exploration mode.
- Model reusability: enabling the reuse of machine learning models across different projects and enterprises.

**Conclusion**
- Integrating large structured data repositories in the enterprise requires building end-to-end scalable workflows.
- Current semi-manual and rule-based systems do not scale and cannot accommodate the continuously growing data.
- Machine learning techniques are an obvious way to go, but multiple practical considerations arise, such as the scarcity of training data and the need to explain the results to business owners.

# Lesen Sie Provost & Fawcett (2013) Kapitel 4.3 und 4.4
- Data science is the study of extracting knowledge from data to make data-driven decisions.
- Data science borrows heavily from the fields of statistics, causal analysis, and data visualization.
- Data science is being applied across industries, most notably in marketing, finance, and retail.
- Data science is more than just data mining algorithms. It requires a fundamental understanding of how to view business problems through a data-oriented lens.
- Data science is often confused with big data, but they are not the same. Big data is simply a term for datasets that are too large for traditional data-processing systems. Big data technologies are often used in the data engineering and processing stages of data science, but they are not required.
- There are a number of fundamental concepts that underlie data science:
    - Extracting useful knowledge from data to solve business problems can be treated systematically by following a process with reasonably well-defined stages.
    - The relationship between the business problem and the analytics solution can often be decomposed into tractable subproblems via the framework of analyzing expected value.
    - Information technology can be used to find informative data items from within a large body of data.
    - Entities that are similar with respect to known features or attributes often are similar with respect to unknown features or attributes.
    - If you look too hard at a set of data, you will find something, but it might not generalize beyond the data you're observing.
    - To draw causal conclusions, one must pay very close attention to the presence of confounding factors, possibly unseen ones.
- There are two main reasons why data science is often misunderstood:
    - There are not many well-established degree programs in data science, so there is not a common understanding of what it entails.
    - Many workers associate their field with the tasks they spend the most time on, rather than the tasks that differentiate their field from others. For example, many data scientists spend a lot of time on data processing, but that is not what differentiates data science from other fields.
- In the early days of data science, most data scientists had to be proficient in the tools of the trade. As data science matures, that will become less of a requirement.
